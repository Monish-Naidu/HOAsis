# We supply policies, we do not draft covenants

Decision taken 2026-08-26. Sits alongside
[parsing-governing-documents.md](./parsing-governing-documents.md) and follows
from the same principle.

## The question

A self-managed board asks for a template. Should we ship one?

## The answer

Split it by layer.

- **Declaration, the CC&Rs: no.** We never supply this text.
- **Bylaws: no.** Same reasoning, slightly weaker, still no.
- **Rules and policies: yes**, as a starter the board edits and adopts by
  recorded vote, naming the statute each one answers to.

## Why the line falls there

**The declaration is recorded against land.** It has to agree with the plat,
with the developer's original grant, and with the allocated interests already
running with each lot. A generic covenant set does not know any of that. An
association that adopts one over an existing declaration has either created a
conflict that the recorded document wins, or recorded something that does not
match its own survey. Neither failure shows up until somebody sells.

**Drafting it is the practice of law, and the words bind somebody's home.**
This is the same objection as auto-detecting violations. A covenant we supplied
becomes the provision an owner is fined under, and "where did this language come
from" has no good answer if the answer is us. The bylaws are a softer version of
the same problem: less permanent, not recorded, still the instrument that
decides whether an election was valid.

**But the rules layer is different in every respect that matters.** The board
already holds the authority to adopt them, granted by a declaration we did not
write. They are not recorded. They can be repealed at the next meeting. And
several states do not merely permit these policies, they **require** a board to
have written ones: a collections policy before enforcing a delinquency, a
records-inspection procedure, a schedule of fines before imposing any. A board
without them is out of compliance, and the honest failure mode of a starter
policy the board edited is much better than the failure mode of no policy at
all.

**The thing boards get wrong here is not the words, it is the existence.** A
self-managed board that has never adopted a collections policy is not going to
draft one from a blank page. It will keep sending letters it cannot defend.
Supplying the shape of the document, with the statute it answers to named, is
the difference between compliance and good intentions.

## What ships, and under what conditions

Every starter policy:

1. **Arrives as a draft.** It lands in the amendment flow as proposed text, not
   as an adopted rule. The board edits it and adopts it by recorded vote, which
   is the act that gives it force.
2. **Names the obligation it answers to**, with the statute, so a board can see
   why it is being offered this rather than trusting that we know.
3. **Says it is not legal advice, and must be checked against the
   declaration.** A rule that goes further than the document above it is void,
   and the rules layer is precisely where that mistake is made.
4. **Is offered for the rules layer only.** The document picker on the amend
   screen offers these when the rules are selected and offers nothing when the
   declaration or the bylaws are.

## What would change this

For the declaration: an attorney in the loop per association, licensed in that
state, reviewing the text before it is recorded. That is a service, not a
feature, and it is a reasonable thing to sell later. It is not something a
template can substitute for.

For the bylaws: a genuine model act. Where a state has adopted UCIOA and
publishes model bylaws, supplying that state's own model with attribution is
defensible in a way that supplying ours is not. Worth revisiting per state, not
in general.
